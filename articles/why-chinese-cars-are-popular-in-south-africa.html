Chinese Cars in South Africa: Why Are They So Popular in 2026?

Chinese cars are everywhere on South African roads. From BYD and Chery to GWM, Haval, Omoda and Jaecoo, here’s why South Africans are suddenly paying attention.

WHAT TO EXPECT

  • You Get More For Your Money
  • The Quality Has Changed
  • Chinese Cars Are Going Crazy With Technology
  • Then There Are The Crazy Car Tests
  • But I’m Not Saying Chinese Cars Are Better
  • Would I Buy One?

I have noticed something mind-boggling on South African roads lately.

Chinese cars.

A few years ago, seeing a Chinese car on South African roads felt like having a foreign tourist come to your village and everyone just stares awkwardly.

Now?

They are impossible to ignore.

BYD. Chery. GWM. Haval. Omoda. Jaecoo. Jetour.

You see them everywhere.

And this isn’t just me imagining things. Chinese brands have been growing rapidly in South Africa. Their share of the passenger-car market increased from 11.2% in 2024 to 16.8% in 2025. By the first quarter of 2026, Chinese brands were approaching a fifth of the local new-vehicle market. (TimesLIVE)

So why the sudden increase?

You Get More For Your Money

This is probably one of the biggest reasons.

Chinese cars are coming packed with features.

I’m talking about bigger screens, cameras, sunroofs, driver-assistance technology, electric windows, electric boots and a whole list of other features.

And sometimes you look at the price and think:

“Wait, that’s all included?”

You can look at some well-established brands and find a fairly basic car at a similar price, while some Chinese competitors are giving you a much longer list of features.

Want the bigger screen?

It’s there.

Want cameras?

There.

Want a sunroof?

Sometimes, it’s there too.

And when you start adding those kinds of features to other cars, the price can climb very quickly.

That’s what makes Chinese cars so interesting to me.

They are giving people a lot of car for their money.

And clearly, South African buyers are noticing.

The Quality Has Changed

Let’s be honest.

Chinese cars didn’t always have the reputation they have today.

But things have changed.

Look at some of the newer Chinese cars and they genuinely look good.

The interiors look modern. The screens look good. The materials feel better and the overall build quality seems much more polished than what many people remember.

I’m still not the biggest fan of some of the body lines though.

Some of them are just doing a little too much for me.

But the interiors?

Nice.

The build quality?

Definitely better than what I remember from years ago.

And this is probably one of the biggest reasons people are starting to take these cars seriously.

They don’t feel like they are trying to compete only on price anymore.

Chinese Cars Are Going Crazy With Technology

Technology might be their secret weapon.

Infotainment screens, driver assistance, cameras, hybrid systems, electric vehicles and fuel-efficient engines are becoming major selling points.

And let’s not even get started on the safety technology.

Some of these cars have so many cameras and sensors that it feels like the car knows what is happening around you before you do.

That technology also changes the driving experience.

They feel easy to drive because the car is constantly helping you.

It almost feels like you’re driving a computer that happens to have four wheels.

And this isn’t just a small trend either. Plug-in hybrid sales in South Africa jumped dramatically in 2025, with Chinese brands helping drive the growth of more affordable plug-in hybrid models. (CleanTechnica)

Then There Are The Crazy Car Tests

This is another thing I find interesting.

I’ve seen videos of Chinese manufacturers putting their cars through some seriously crazy tests.

Crash tests.

Water tests.

Extreme manoeuvres.

Tests designed to show what these cars can actually handle.

I even recently saw a video of a car performing what looked like a barrel roll in a tunnel.

I just sat there thinking:

“Why are they doing this?”

But at the same time…

That’s pretty cool.

And I think that’s part of the appeal.

Chinese manufacturers aren’t just saying:

“Our car is affordable.”

They’re also trying to show people:

“Look what this thing can do.”

Obviously, one crazy demonstration doesn’t prove that every Chinese car is going to last 20 years without a problem.

But it definitely gets people’s attention.

But I’m Not Saying Chinese Cars Are Better

Let’s make that clear.

I’m not saying Chinese cars are automatically better than Toyota, Volkswagen, Ford or any other established brand.

They still have things to prove.

Reliability over many years matters.

Resale value matters.

Parts availability matters.

After-sales support matters.

Established brands have spent decades building trust with South African drivers.

Chinese brands are still building that trust.

But I think it would be a mistake to ignore them.

The numbers are already showing that South Africans are giving them a chance. In the first half of 2026, several Chinese brands recorded strong sales growth, while brands such as Chery, Jetour and GWM continued climbing the local rankings. (TopAuto)

And that’s the part I find most interesting.

Would I Buy One?

If I had to get one myself right now?

The BYD Shark 6.

That thing is beautiful.

I’ve seen it and honestly, it is one of those cars that makes you stop and look.

And that’s probably the biggest change of all.

A few years ago, many South Africans would see a Chinese car and ask:

“What is that?”

Now people are asking:

“How much is it?”

That’s a big difference.

Chinese cars aren’t some strange new thing on South African roads anymore.

They’re becoming a serious part of the market.

And whether you love them or hate them, one thing is becoming very difficult to argue with:

Chinese car brands have arrived in South Africa.
