How to Pass Matric Maths Easily: 12 Tips That Actually Work

Struggling with matric Maths? I was once there too. I went from 47% to 76% by changing how I studied. Here are the Maths study strategies that helped me improve.

How to Pass Matric Maths Easily (Even If You’re Bad at Maths)

How to pass matric Maths? If you’re a high school student struggling with Mathematics, don’t skip this.

I have been a high school student too, and trust me, it wasn’t always easy for me.

For a long time, my problem wasn’t that I couldn’t do Mathematics. My problem was how I was studying it.

I used to study Maths like a subject where I could just read my notes, remember a few things and hope for the best in the exam.

That did not work.

Things started changing when I changed my perspective and, more importantly, my way of studying Mathematics.

I went from getting around 47% to 76% in my final exam.

So if you’re currently struggling with matric Maths, here are the things that helped me.

1. Stop reading Maths notes and start practising

This was probably the biggest change I made.

Maths is a practice subject.

You can read your textbook for three hours and still struggle when the actual question is in front of you.

Reading helps you understand the concept, but solving questions is what trains you to actually do Mathematics.

It’s okay to watch YouTube videos when you don’t understand something. But don’t make the mistake of watching someone solve 20 questions while you sit there thinking:

“Yeah, I understand this.”

Try this instead.

Watch the first example.

Pause the video.

Now take out your own paper and solve the question yourself.

After that, check your answer.

Then move on to the next example and try it yourself before watching the solution.

You’re not studying Maths by watching someone else do it.

You are studying Maths when you can do it yourself.

2. Start using past papers early

Don’t wait until three days before your Maths exam to suddenly download 10 past papers.

That’s panic, not preparation.

Start using past papers well before the exam.

Past papers can help you identify:

  • Topics you don’t understand
  • Question types you struggle with
  • Mistakes you keep making
  • How much time you spend on questions
  • Areas where you are losing marks

You can also use official NSC past papers and memos from the Department of Basic Education.

But don’t just do a past paper and check your percentage.

Study your mistakes.

If you got a question wrong, ask yourself:

Why did I get this wrong?

Was it because you didn’t know the formula?

Did you misunderstand the question?

Did you make an algebra mistake?

Did you use the wrong method?

That answer is more valuable than simply knowing that you got the question wrong.

3. Don’t cram Maths answers

Please don’t memorise an answer just because you saw it in a past paper.

You might get the exact same question.

But what happens when the numbers change?

You’re cooked.

Instead of memorising the answer, learn the method and the steps.

Ask yourself:

What type of question is this?

What method do I use?

What steps do I follow?

Once you understand the method, you can handle different versions of the same question.

That’s much more useful than memorising:

“The answer is 24.”

4. Learn how to use your formulas

Knowing that a formula exists isn’t enough.

You need to know:

  • What the formula is used for
  • When you should use it
  • What each variable means
  • How to substitute correctly
  • How to work through the calculation

Don’t just stare at the formula sheet.

Use the formulas.

Take different questions and practise identifying which formula you need.

Eventually, when you see a question, you should start thinking:

“Okay, this is the type of question where I need this formula.”

That is when formulas become useful.

5. Break Maths into smaller topics

One of the worst things you can tell yourself is:

“Today I’m going to study Maths.”

That’s too broad.

There are too many topics.

Instead, give yourself a specific target.

For example:

Monday: Functions

Tuesday: Finance

Wednesday: Trigonometry

Thursday: Analytical Geometry

Friday: Statistics

Now your brain has one specific job.

You don’t have to conquer the entire Maths syllabus in one sitting.

Break it down.

It’s much easier to say:

“Today I’m doing Functions.”

than:

“Today I’m doing Maths.”

6. Start with the topics you struggle with

Don’t spend your entire study session doing questions you already know because they make you feel good.

If you’re already getting 80% on Functions but 35% on Trigonometry, spending another five hours on Functions probably isn’t the best use of your time.

Find your weak areas.

Then attack them.

You don’t have to be perfect immediately.

Start with easier questions.

Understand the basics.

Then move to medium questions.

Then attempt harder ones.

Build your way up.

7. Learn how marks are awarded

This is something I wish more students understood.

In Mathematics, your working matters.

Don’t just write an answer and move on.

Show your calculations clearly.

If you make a mistake later in the question, your working can also help you understand where things went wrong.

When practising, get into the habit of showing your steps just like you would in the exam.

Don’t only practise getting the final answer.

Practise earning the marks.

8. Don’t leave the paper blank

Now, I’m not saying you should randomly write anything and expect the examiner to give you marks.

I’m saying don’t give up immediately when a question looks difficult.

Read it again.

Write down what you know.

Write down the relevant formula.

Look at the information given in the question.

Try to work out what the question is asking.

Start with something.

Sometimes the first step is enough to get you moving.

And if you genuinely don’t know how to continue, move on and come back later.

Don’t spend 20 minutes fighting one question while easier marks are waiting for you somewhere else.

9. Practise under time pressure

Being able to solve a Maths question is one thing.

Being able to solve it during an exam with a clock running is another thing.

When you start becoming comfortable with the topics, time yourself.

Do a section without constantly checking your phone or looking at the memo.

Eventually, try doing a full past paper under proper exam conditions.

Then check:

Did I finish?

Where did I waste time?

Which questions took too long?

Which questions did I rush?

The goal isn’t just to know Mathematics.

You also need to know how to manage your time.

10. Ask the smart students for help

This helped me too.

Sometimes your teacher explains something one way, but another student explains it in a way that suddenly makes everything click.

So don’t be afraid to ask someone who understands the topic.

Ask them:

“How did you get this answer?”

But don’t just copy their work.

Ask them to explain why they did each step.

Then try a similar question yourself.

If you can solve it without their help, you’ve actually learned something.

11. Go back and redo the questions you got wrong

This is one of the easiest ways to see whether you’re actually improving.

Let’s say you got five questions wrong today.

Don’t just look at the memo and forget about them.

Write down the questions you struggled with.

Come back a few days later.

Try them again without looking at the solution.

If you can now solve them, that’s progress.

If you still can’t, you know exactly what you need to work on.

Your mistakes can become your study guide.

12. Don’t try to learn everything the night before

We’ve all been there.

It’s 11 PM.

The exam is tomorrow.

Suddenly you decide you’re going to learn the entire Maths syllabus overnight.

Good luck.

Don’t do that.

Mathematics is much easier to improve when you practise consistently.

Even if you only have a limited amount of time, having a focused study session every day is better than trying to force the entire syllabus into your head the night before.

Small progress every day adds up.

The biggest lesson I learned

Going from 47% to 76% didn’t happen because I suddenly became a Mathematics genius.

I changed how I approached the subject.

I stopped treating Maths like something I could simply read.

I started practising.

I started using past papers.

I started paying attention to my mistakes.

I stopped memorising answers and started learning methods.

I broke the syllabus into smaller topics.

And I started asking for help when I didn’t understand something.

That’s what made the difference for me.

And finally… pray.

I’m not going to pretend studying is the only thing that matters.

If you’re a person of faith, pray.

Ask God for wisdom, discipline and understanding.

But don’t pray for the marks and then refuse to open the textbook.

Pray, then put in the work.

You don’t have to be the smartest person in your class to pass matric Maths.

You need a strategy, consistent practice and the willingness to learn from your mistakes.

You can improve.

Now go do some Maths.

Quick Matric Maths Checklist

Before your next exam, ask yourself:

  • Have I practised instead of only reading?
  • Have I used past papers?
  • Do I know my weak topics?
  • Have I redone questions I previously got wrong?
  • Do I know when to use my formulas?
  • Can I solve questions without looking at the memo?
  • Have I practised under time pressure?
  • Do I show all my working?
  • Have I asked for help where I’m stuck?
  • Have I actually put in the work?

Don’t just study harder. Study smarter.
