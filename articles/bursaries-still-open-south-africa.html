Bursaries Still Open for 2027 in South Africa

Looking for university funding? Here are the bursaries still open for 2027 in South Africa, their closing dates, and how to apply.

  • 1. Government Funding: NSFAS and ISFAP
  • 2. Teaching Bursaries: Funza Lushaka
  • 3. Corporate and Postgraduate Bursaries (Closing Nov – Dec)
  • What Documents Do You Need to Apply?
  • Final Advice: Submit Even If Your Marks Aren't Perfect

If you still need funding for university or college in 2027, you have not missed your chance yet. Several major South African bursaries are still accepting applications right now, but many close before the end of November and December.

Whether you are in matric or already studying at university, securing a bursary takes off the heavy burden of tuition, books, and living expenses. Here is the full list of bursaries that are still open, their exact closing dates, and how to apply before the doors shut.

Top Bursaries Still Open for 2027 Applications

Every year, thousands of students assume all bursary applications close in the middle of winter. In reality, some of South Africa's largest funding schemes accept applications right up to the start of the new academic year.

Here are the biggest bursary schemes you can still apply for today:

Bursary Programme Closing Date Who Can Apply What It Covers
NSFAS 2027 31 October 2026 Households earning under R350,000 / year Full tuition, accommodation, meals, book allowance
ISFAP Missing Middle 31 October 2026 Households earning R350,000 – R600,000 / year Full tuition, residence, food, personal allowance
Funza Lushaka (Returning) 30 November 2026 Returning B.Ed and PGCE teaching students Tuition, accommodation, study material
DSTI-NRF Honours Funding 23 November 2026 Postgraduate Honours (Science & Humanities) Full-cost or partial-cost study support
Milpark Bursary Programme 10 December 2026 Commerce, banking, and business students Tuition and examination fees
PSG Bursary Programme 20 December 2026 IT, Finance, Actuarial Science, Engineering Tuition, textbooks, and mentoring
Funza Lushaka (New Students) 31 January 2027 First-year teaching students (priority subjects) Comprehensive tuition, meals, and living allowance
Urgent Deadline Alert: Both NSFAS and ISFAP close on 31 October 2026. If you have not submitted your online application yet, do it today to ensure your household income documents are verified in time.

1. Government Funding: NSFAS and ISFAP

The official NSFAS portal handles government funding for students from low-income families. If your combined household income is R350,000 or less per year, NSFAS pays your full university fees, accommodation, and monthly food allowance.

If your family earns slightly too much for NSFAS—between R350,000 and R600,000 annually—you qualify as "missing middle." The Ikusasa Student Financial Aid Programme (ISFAP) was built specifically to fund these students in high-demand fields like medicine, engineering, and accounting.

2. Teaching Bursaries: Funza Lushaka

If you plan to study education, the Funza Lushaka Bursary is one of the best funding options in South Africa. It covers tuition, accommodation, books, and gives you a monthly living allowance.

In exchange, you agree to teach at a public school for the same number of years you received funding. Returning bursars must apply on the Funza Lushaka website by 30 November 2026, while brand new teaching students have until 31 January 2027.

3. Corporate and Postgraduate Bursaries (Closing Nov – Dec)

Several private companies and national research bodies keep their funding open through late summer. The National Research Foundation (NRF) closes its honours funding in late November, while private firms like PSG and Milpark accept applications well into December.

Many local municipalities also publish their mayoral bursaries between November and January once matric exams wrap up. Keep an eye on your local municipal website or visit your town hall to pick up physical forms.

What Documents Do You Need to Apply?

Do not wait until the closing day to look for your paperwork. Gather these certified copies right now:

  • Your South African green barcoded ID or smart ID card.
  • Certified ID copies of your parents or legal guardians.
  • Proof of household income (latest payslips, pension slips, or a SASSA letter).
  • An official affidavit if your parents are unemployed or single.
  • Your latest Grade 12 results or current university transcript.

When preparing your documents, following our checklist of proven bursary application steps will prevent simple errors that cause applications to get rejected.

Final Advice: Submit Even If Your Marks Aren't Perfect

Many students do not apply because they think their marks are too low. However, bursaries like NSFAS care primarily about household financial need rather than straight-A report cards.

Upload clear, readable scans, check your email daily for status updates, and never pay anyone money to submit a bursary application for you.
