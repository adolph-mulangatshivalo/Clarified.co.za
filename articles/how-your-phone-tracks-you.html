Why Your Phone Knows So Much About You

Your phone knows where you go, what you search for and what you watch. Here's how phones, apps and social media collect your data and build a picture of you.

Think about your phone for a second. Have you ever wondered exactly how your phone tracks you?

It knows where you go. It knows who you talk to. It knows what you search for, what videos you watch, what apps you use and, in some cases, what you are interested in buying.

And somehow, after searching for something once, you might suddenly start seeing adverts for it everywhere.

It can feel like your phone is reading your mind. It isn't. But the amount of information your phone and the apps on it can collect is pretty crazy.

How Does Your Phone Know Where You Are?

One of the main ways how your phone tracks you is by working out your location using GPS, Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth and nearby cellphone towers.

This is useful when you open Google Maps and need directions. It is also useful when an app needs to show you something nearby. But location data can reveal much more than just a dot on a map.

If your phone regularly sees you at the same place every morning and another place every afternoon, it can help build a picture of your daily routine. Your location can show places you visit, how often you visit them and how long you normally stay there.

That is a lot of information from something as simple as your phone knowing where you are.

What Information Do Apps Collect?

When you install an app, it may ask for permissions: Camera, Microphone, Contacts, Location, Photos, and Bluetooth.

Some of these permissions make sense. A camera app needs your camera. Google Maps needs your location. WhatsApp needs access to your contacts if you want to find people you know.

But not every app needs access to everything. That is why you should actually check what permissions your apps have.

If a basic calculator app wants access to your microphone, location and contacts, that should make you ask: Why?

Can Your Phone See What You Search For?

Your search history can reveal your interests, problems, plans and questions.

Search for laptops and you have probably been researching laptops. Search for flights to Cape Town and there is a good chance you are thinking about travelling. Search for â€œhow to fix my carâ€ and your phone has another little piece of information about you.

One search might not mean much. But thousands of searches over months or years can create a surprisingly detailed picture of a person. And this is where things get interesting.

Is Your Phone Listening to You?

One of the biggest privacy questions people have is: â€œIs my phone listening to me?â€

You have probably experienced this. You talk to someone about something, then later you see an advert related to it. It is easy to think: â€œThere's no way that's a coincidence. My phone heard me.â€

But targeted advertising does not necessarily require your phone to record your conversations. Companies can learn a lot from other data.

Your searches, websites, location, purchases, app activity, interests and the people or content you interact with can all help create a profile of you. So sometimes the technology looks like it is reading your mind when it is actually making a very educated guess. That is arguably even more interesting.

How Your Phone Learns Your Daily Habits

Modern smartphones are not just storing information. They can also recognise patterns.

For example, your phone might learn that you normally:

  • Wake up around 6:30
  • Leave home around 7:15
  • Spend the day at university or work
  • Use certain apps during lunch
  • Travel home in the afternoon
  • Watch YouTube at night
  • Charge your phone before going to sleep

None of these things individually seems very important. Put them together, and they describe your life surprisingly well.

This is one reason data is so valuable. A single piece of information can be boring. Millions of pieces of information can become a story.

How Social Media Tracks Your Behaviour

This is where things get even bigger.

Every time you like a post, watch a video, follow someone, search for something or spend time looking at a particular type of content, you are giving the platform information about your interests.

Even something as simple as watching a video until the end can be a signal. If you keep watching football videos but quickly scroll past cooking videos, the platform learns that you are probably more interested in football.

Do this thousands of times and the recommendations become extremely accurate. That is why TikTok, YouTube and Instagram can sometimes show you exactly what you want to see. They are not guessing randomly â€” they have data.

Why Do Companies Want Your Data?

Because information is useful.

Companies can use data to:

  • Personalise your experience
  • Recommend content
  • Show advertisements
  • Understand their users
  • Detect suspicious activity
  • Improve their products
  • Predict what users might want

For advertising companies, knowing what people are interested in can be extremely valuable.

An advertiser would rather show a laptop advert to someone searching for laptops than randomly show it to someone who has no interest in computers. Better targeting can mean better results.

So, How Much Does Your Phone Really Know About You?

Not exactly.

Your phone itself is just one part of a much bigger system. Your phone, apps, websites, social media platforms, advertising networks and online accounts can all collect different pieces of information.

The real issue is what happens when these pieces are combined:

  • Your location tells someone where you go.
  • Your searches show what you are interested in.
  • Your purchases show what you spend money on.
  • Your social media activity shows what content you like.
  • Your apps show what services you use.

Together, these things can create a detailed digital profile. And that profile can tell companies things about you that you may never have directly told them.

How to Stop Your Phone Tracking You

You don't have to throw your phone into the Vaal River and go live in the mountains.

There are simple, practical steps you can take to protect your privacy:

  • Check your app permissions regularly.
  • Turn off location access for apps that don't need it.
  • Delete apps you no longer use.
  • Review your Google and social media privacy settings.
  • Be careful about which apps you install.
  • Think before giving an app access to your contacts, microphone, camera or photos.

And remember that free apps are not always really â€œfreeâ€. Sometimes you are paying with your attention. Sometimes you are paying with your data.

The Weirdest Part: Connecting the Dots

The scary part isn't necessarily that your phone knows where you are. It's how much can be figured out from small pieces of information.

Your phone doesn't need to know everything about you. It just needs enough information to connect the dots. And once those dots are connected, your digital life can reveal a lot more about you than you might expect.

The next time your phone recommends something that feels way too accurate, remember: it probably isn't reading your mind. It has just been watching the patterns.
