High-Demand Degrees in South Africa: 2026 Career Guide

Every year, the Department of Higher Education and Training (DHET) publishes its National List of Occupations in High Demand (OIHD). This report tracks the exact career skills South African businesses, hospitals, banks, and public institutions desperately need.

If you are a high school matriculant, a current university student, or a parent planning tuition, this comprehensive guide highlights the high demand degrees in South Africa that offer strong job security, solid starting salaries, and long-term career growth.

Key Fact: According to Stats SA graduate surveys, university graduates in South Africa experience an unemployment rate of under 10%â€”far lower than the national average. Choosing an in-demand degree lowers that risk even further.

Why Your Choice of Degree Matters More Than Ever in 2026

Gone are the days when simply having any generic degree guaranteed a desk job after graduation. Employers today look for practical, technical, and analytical qualifications that solve immediate business problems.

Certain professions consistently face shortages of qualified graduates in South Africa. When supply is low and demand is high, employers compete for talent by offering higher starting salaries and quicker promotions.

To help fund your studies in these key fields, you should also check our guide on how to apply for bursaries in South Africa, as corporate bursary sponsors heavily favor in-demand degrees.

1. Computer Science and Software Engineering

South Africaâ€™s digital economy is expanding rapidly across banking, insurance, retail, and telecommunications. Major local banks like Standard Bank, Capitec, and Discovery hire hundreds of software developers, cybersecurity analysts, and cloud architects each year.

A Bachelor of Science (BSc) in Computer Science or a Bachelor of Engineering (BEng) in Software Engineering prepares you to build enterprise systems, mobile applications, and secure networks.

Graduates entering this sector regularly earn starting salaries between R28,000 and R42,000 per month, with seasoned senior engineers reaching well into six figures. You can also explore our review of the 7 in-demand tech skills South African companies want.

  • Key Roles: Full-Stack Software Developer, Cloud Engineer, Cybersecurity Analyst.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 34 to 42 points (requires pure Mathematics Level 6 or higher).
  • Top Institutions: University of the Witwatersrand (Wits), University of Cape Town (UCT), University of Pretoria (UP), and Stellenbosch University.

2. Data Science and Business Analytics

Modern businesses generate massive streams of customer data, transaction records, and market metrics every second. Companies need skilled specialists who can turn raw data into profitable business decisions.

Degrees in Data Science, Statistics, and Mathematical Sciences are among the fastest-growing qualifications in the country. Because machine learning and artificial intelligence are revolutionizing local commerce, data talent is in massive demand.

Starting pay for junior data scientists and analysts typically ranges from R30,000 to R45,000 per month. If you want to see how these careers stack up against other fields, take a look at which degrees lead to the highest-paying jobs in South Africa.

  • Key Roles: Data Scientist, Business Intelligence Developer, Machine Learning Specialist.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 35 to 40 points.
  • Core Subjects: Pure Mathematics Level 6, Information Technology, or AP Maths.

3. Engineering: Civil, Electrical, and Renewable Energy

South Africaâ€™s ongoing power grid upgrades, water treatment projects, and solar farm developments mean qualified engineers remain a fixture on the national scarce skills list.

Graduates with accredited qualifications recognized by the Engineering Council of South Africa (ECSA) find work across consulting firms, mining houses, state utilities, and independent power producers.

For an in-depth breakdown of engineering compensation packages, see our dedicated guide on the 10 highest paying engineering jobs in South Africa.

  • Key Specializations: Electrical Engineering, Renewable Energy, Civil Infrastructure, and Mechanical Engineering.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 36 to 42 points (requires Physical Sciences and Pure Mathematics at Level 6 or above).
  • Starting Pay: R27,000 to R40,000 per month depending on the industry.

4. Accounting and Financial Sciences (CA Track)

Chartered Accountants in South Africa, designated as CA(SA), remain among the most respected and employable professionals in the nation. The South African Institute of Chartered Accountants (SAICA) sets rigorous standards that are admired globally.

Students who complete a SAICA-accredited Bachelor of Accounting Science or BCom Accounting degree followed by their CTA (Certificate in the Theory of Accounting) rarely struggle to find employment.

Large audit firms (PwC, Deloitte, EY, KPMG) and multinational corporations absorb hundreds of trainee accountants into 3-year training contracts every single year.

  • Key Roles: Trainee Accountant, Financial Analyst, Internal Auditor, Tax Consultant.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 32 to 38 points (pure Mathematics Level 5 or 6 required; Mathematical Literacy is usually excluded).
  • Career Trajectory: Trainee salaries start around R18,000 to R26,000 per month during articles, jumping to R55,000+ per month immediately after qualification.

5. Healthcare, Medicine, and Pharmacy

The healthcare system in South Africaâ€”both in state hospitals and the private sectorâ€”faces a permanent shortage of medical doctors, specialized nurses, and clinical pharmacists.

A Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBChB) guarantees immediate placement into mandatory paid two-year medical internship and one-year community service programmes with the Department of Health.

Pharmacy graduates (BPharm) and nursing science graduates also enjoy near-100% employment rates across retail pharmacies, public clinics, and private hospital groups.

  • Key Roles: Medical Doctor, Clinical Pharmacist, Registered Nurse, Sonographer.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 38 to 45 points (extremely competitive entry).
  • Starting Pay: Medical interns earn approximately R45,000 to R55,000 per month (inclusive of allowances).

6. Supply Chain and Logistics Management

South Africa is the economic gateway to Southern Africa, boasting major commercial ports in Durban, Cape Town, and Port Elizabeth, alongside massive freight corridors.

The rise of regional e-commerce, automated warehousing, and global trade challenges has elevated Supply Chain Management into one of the country's hottest commercial qualifications.

A BCom in Supply Chain Management or Logistics equips students to manage distribution networks, inventory, and cross-border customs operations.

  • Key Roles: Supply Chain Planner, Logistics Coordinator, Procurement Specialist.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 28 to 34 points.
  • Starting Pay: R20,000 to R32,000 per month.

7. STEM Education and High School Teaching

South Africa has an urgent, well-documented shortage of qualified high school educators who can teach Mathematics, Physical Sciences, and Technical subjects.

A Bachelor of Education (BEd) degree specializing in the Senior Phase and Further Education and Training (FET) phase provides guaranteed career openings, especially in growing secondary schools across both public and independent school sectors.

In addition, the Funza Lushaka Bursary programme fully funds tuition, accommodation, and allowances for eligible students who commit to teaching STEM subjects after graduating.

  • Key Roles: Mathematics Teacher, Physical Sciences Teacher, Coding & Robotics Educator.
  • Typical APS Requirement: 26 to 32 points.
  • Starting Pay: R22,000 to R28,000 per month on government entry scales (REQV 14).

Key High School Subjects You Need to Protect

One of the biggest hurdles South African learners face when applying to university is subject choice. Many students drop pure Mathematics for Mathematical Literacy in Grade 10 without realizing they are disqualifying themselves from engineering, computer science, medicine, and accounting.

If you want to keep your doors open to high-demand careers, protecting your pure Mathematics mark is vital by mastering proven matric maths exam techniques.

You can verify current national workforce shortages directly on the Department of Higher Education and Training (DHET) portal and review the critical skills guidelines published on the official South African Government portal.

Step-by-Step Checklist for Picking Your Degree

  1. Calculate your realistic APS: Use your latest Grade 11 or Grade 12 results to see which faculties you realistically qualify for.
  2. Check the subject prerequisites: Never assume a university will admit you with Mathematical Literacy for a science or commerce degree.
  3. Review graduate hiring data: Confirm that the qualification appears on the DHET Occupations in High Demand list.
  4. Apply to at least 3 institutions: Apply early to a mix of traditional universities and universities of technology to protect yourself against faculty caps.
  5. Secure funding early: Submit your application via the official NSFAS portal and apply for private bursaries well before closing deadlines.
